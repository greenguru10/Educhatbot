# Physics: Electromagnetism, Electric Fields, and Maxwell's Equations

## Coulomb's Law and Electric Potential
Coulomb's Law states that the electrostatic force $F$ between two point charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ separated by distance $r$ in vacuum is:
$$F = \frac{1}{4\pi \varepsilon_0} \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$$
where $\varepsilon_0 \approx 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ F/m}$ is the vacuum permittivity.
The electric field vector $\vec{E}$ at a point in space is defined as the force per unit positive test charge: $\vec{E} = \frac{\vec{F}}{q_0}$.

## Maxwell's Four Equations of Electromagnetism
Maxwell's equations unify electricity, magnetism, and optics into a single foundational theory:

1. **Gauss's Law for Electricity**: Electric flux through a closed surface is proportional to the enclosed electric charge:
$$\nabla \cdot \vec{E} = \frac{\rho}{\varepsilon_0}$$

2. **Gauss's Law for Magnetism**: Net magnetic flux through any closed Gaussian surface is zero (magnetic monopoles do not exist):
$$\nabla \cdot \vec{B} = 0$$

3. **Faraday's Law of Induction**: A time-varying magnetic field induces an electromotive force (spatially circulating electric field):
$$\nabla \times \vec{E} = -\frac{\partial \vec{B}}{\partial t}$$

4. **Ampère-Maxwell Law**: Magnetic fields are generated by electric currents and time-varying electric displacement fields:
$$\nabla \times \vec{B} = \mu_0 \vec{J} + \mu_0 \varepsilon_0 \frac{\partial \vec{E}}{\partial t}$$

These equations mathematically predict the propagation of electromagnetic waves in vacuum at the speed of light $c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0}} \approx 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$.
