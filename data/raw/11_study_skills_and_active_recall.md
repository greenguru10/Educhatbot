# Study Skills: Spaced Repetition, Active Recall, and Feynman Technique

## Active Recall vs Passive Review
Cognitive psychology demonstrates that passive studying (re-reading notes, highlighting textbooks) leads to an illusion of competence.
- **Active Recall**: The practice of stimulating memory retrieval through self-testing, flashcards, or solving practice problems without looking at the answer key. Retrieval strengthens neural synapses and solidifies long-term memory.

## Spaced Repetition and The Forgetting Curve
Hermann Ebbinghaus demonstrated that human memory retention exponentially decays over time (the Forgetting Curve).
- **Spaced Repetition Systems (SRS)**: Reviewing material at strategically increasing time intervals (e.g. Day 1, Day 3, Day 7, Day 21) flattens the forgetting curve and maximizes long-term retention efficiency.

## The Feynman Technique
A four-step mental framework for mastering difficult academic concepts:
1. **Choose a Concept**: Identify the specific principle or topic you want to understand.
2. **Teach it to a Child**: Explain the concept using simple, non-jargon language and intuitive analogies.
3. **Identify Gaps**: Pinpoint exactly where your explanation breaks down or becomes vague, and return to the primary literature to clarify.
4. **Simplify and Refine**: Re-explain the concept using concise definitions and concrete examples.
